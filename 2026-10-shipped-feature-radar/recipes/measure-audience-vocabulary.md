# Measure your audience's vocabulary before you write a reviewer

1. List the words your own copy leans on, grouped by idea: moving data, structuring it, monitoring, analysis, AI, systems, units of work, platform words.
2. Pick at least three independent sources, and at least one where the writers have never used your product: public job posts at target accounts, practitioner articles and talks, support chats, call transcripts.
3. Run one agent per source in parallel. Each writes a full table to a file and returns a short summary. Ask each for N, counts per term, the meaning the writer intended, and up to three short snippets with a source.
4. Separate voices. Count your own staff, sales-rep paraphrase, and users repeating your UI labels apart from outside words.
5. Put a 95% Wilson interval on every key proportion. A zero count still has an upper bound (0 of 63 is 0 to 6%).
6. Write verdicts: AVOID, EXPLAIN, USE. Mark confidence High only when two independent sources agree and one of them is a non-user source.
7. Write down the bias of each source next to its numbers. Job posts under-report everyday chores; customers echo your product's words.
8. Re-run when a new source arrives. Update counts and confidence, not just verdicts.
