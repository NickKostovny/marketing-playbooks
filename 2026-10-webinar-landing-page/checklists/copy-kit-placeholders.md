# The copy kit: what was supplied, what was bracketed

The brief asked for six deliverables and said: use only what I provide, make nothing up. The honest way to ship that is to write the whole kit with brackets where the inputs are missing, and list the brackets at the top.

## Deliverables

1. Landing page copy: headline, subhead, three "what you'll learn" bullets, speakers, a point-of-view block, a register CTA.
2. Speaker graphic copy for a horizontal layout: eyebrow, title, one tile per speaker, footer with the link.
3. Three LinkedIn posts: announcement, a point-of-view teaser, a last-call reminder.
4. A first-person post for our speaker.
5. A short email invite.

## Brackets at the start of the day

| Placeholder | Resolved by |
| --- | --- |
| Date and time | The publication's listing, same morning |
| Two vendor speakers and titles | One from the listing; one from the owner, late afternoon, with a bio written from his public profile and marked as such |
| Our speaker's title | The listing |
| A customer quote from a reference video | Still open. The teaser post runs without it; the note on the post says to swap the opener for the quote when it arrives. |
| Approved time-savings numbers | Still open. Nothing on the page claims a number. |
| The sales lead's point-of-view draft | Still open, then the section was cut (see the cut sheet). A stand-in paragraph was marked PLACEHOLDER on the page while it existed. |

## Rule

A placeholder on a page is a visible word, PLACEHOLDER, in the page's own type, not lorem ipsum and not an invented sentence that reads as final. Reviewers notice the word. They do not notice a plausible fake.
