# One-pager restraint

Use this checklist when you build or review a sales one-pager. Each pattern here makes a page look generated. Each one has a replacement, so removing it does not leave a hole.

| If you see | Ask | Replace with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A mono all-caps eyebrow over a section | Does the section need a label at all? | A short sentence-case label in secondary ink, or nothing |
| An accent rule or bar next to a label or quote | What does the bar mean? | Nothing |
| "01 / 02 / 03" on columns | Does the order matter? | No numerals. Keep numerals only for steps that must happen in sequence. |
| A stat strip of big numbers | What is each number, and what changed? | One sentence per outcome, then one line of what changed |
| Icon tiles next to product names | Does the icon say something the name does not? | A table: name, one-line job |
| A wall of partner or source logos | Can a reader verify it? Will it go out of date? | One sentence that names the categories |
| A quote with a glyph, avatar, and accent bar | Is the quote strong enough alone? | The quote, a role, a company tier |
| A dark hero band | Is this a web page? | The headline carries the top. Use at most one closing dark band. |
| An italic or colored word in the headline | Does the emphasis add meaning? | A plain headline |
| Decorative geometry or a watermark | What does it say? | Nothing |
| 0.5px lines | Will it print? | 1px solid lines |

## The structure test

- [ ] Each side has one job. For a booth handout: the front says what it is and shows proof; the back answers the enterprise objections and says how to start.
- [ ] A reader at the table gets the front's headline in three seconds.
- [ ] The product architecture is drawn as it works (layers, shared foundation), not as a feature list.
- [ ] Every caption is written from what the image actually shows.
- [ ] During a redesign, the copy changes only where the layout forces it. Otherwise reviewers cannot tell what they are reacting to.
